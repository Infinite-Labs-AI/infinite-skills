# X Article Writer

Write X-native longform that has a strong hook, a clear point, and enough receipts to feel real.

## Before Writing

Look for source material first:

- Founder story, customer story, product launch, teardown, or lesson.
- Numbers, screenshots, timelines, tools, customer quotes, experiments, or revenue data.
- Prior draft, notes, tweet thread, launch notes, or article outline.
- Audience: founders, growth marketers, operators, creators, investors, or customers.

If there are no receipts, ask for proof before making big claims. Do not invent numbers, quotes, screenshots, or customer examples.

## Choose The Angle

Pick one main angle:

- **Result:** what happened and why it matters.
- **Mistake:** what went wrong and what changed.
- **Teardown:** what a product, funnel, launch, or campaign reveals.
- **Contrarian take:** what the market gets wrong.
- **Playbook:** how to do something specific.
- **Launch story:** why this product or feature exists now.

The angle should be easy to say in one sentence. If it needs a paragraph to explain, sharpen it.

## Write The Hook

Create 5-10 hook options before drafting. A good hook usually has one of:

- A specific number.
- A surprising contrast.
- A concrete scene.
- A strong opinion.
- A named company, product, tool, or channel.
- A useful promise.

Avoid polite blog intros. The first few lines should make the right reader want the rest.

## Draft The Article

Use this shape:

1. Hook: the reason to keep reading.
2. Context: what happened, who it affects, why now.
3. Tension: what most people assume or miss.
4. Mechanism: what actually caused the result.
5. Details: numbers, examples, tools, steps, screenshots, or customer language.
6. Takeaway: the useful lesson or belief shift.
7. Close: a sharp final line, not a generic summary.

Keep paragraphs short. Use simple language. Make every section add new information.

## Rewrite A Draft

When rewriting:

- Preserve the facts, not the structure.
- Move the strongest proof into the first screen.
- Cut generic startup advice.
- Replace abstract claims with examples.
- Make the title and opener stronger before polishing the body.
- Keep the voice human, direct, and a little opinionated.
