# Positioning

Find a clear position: who this is for, what problem it owns, and why it is different.

## Gather Context

Read existing product and market material first. If essential context is missing, ask for:

- Product capability and strongest current use case.
- Best customer or desired first customer.
- Main alternatives and the status quo.
- Proof already available.
- Business constraint: price point, sales motion, geography, compliance, founder appetite.

## Diagnose The Current Position

Name the current position in one sentence. Then test it:

- Is the customer specific enough to find?
- Is the pain urgent enough to act on?
- Is the alternative named?
- Is the product’s advantage provable?
- Would competitors be happy to say the same thing?

If the current angle is too broad, split it into smaller positioning options.

## Create Positioning Options

Create 3-5 positioning options. Each option must include:

- **Target:** a reachable customer group.
- **Moment:** when the need becomes active.
- **Enemy:** the current way they solve it.
- **Advantage:** what this product does better or differently.
- **Proof:** what can make the claim credible.
- **Tradeoff:** who this position excludes.

Prefer options based on behavior, urgency, data access, workflow ownership, distribution access, or regulatory pressure.

## Pick The Best Position

Score each option from 1-5 on:

- Reachability.
- Pain intensity.
- Competitive contrast.
- Proof strength.
- Revenue potential.
- Speed to test.

Recommend one lead position and one backup position. Do not pick a position only because it sounds large.
