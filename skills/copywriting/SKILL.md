# Copywriting

Write clear website and landing-page copy that makes a strong promise and backs it up with proof.

## Scope

Use this for:

- Homepage, landing page, pricing page, feature page, product page, and about page copy.
- Hero headline, subheadline, CTA, value proposition, tagline, section copy, and objection handling.
- Rewriting weak page copy to make it clearer and more persuasive.

Do not use this for:

- Email flows or lifecycle messages. Use `email-sequence`.
- Blog posts, SEO articles, or content calendars. Use `content-strategy` or `seo-strategy`.
- Longform X/Twitter articles. Use `x-article-writer`.
- Platform-specific Google, Meta, or LinkedIn ad copy. Use `creative-brief` or `paid-ads`.

Before asking questions, read existing product-marketing context if present: `.agents/product-marketing.md`, `.claude/product-marketing.md`, or `product-marketing-context.md`.

## Copy Inputs

Look for or ask for:

- Target reader and traffic source.
- Desired action.
- Offer or product being sold.
- Current page or draft.
- Proof: numbers, customers, testimonials, demos, screenshots, case studies.
- Objections and alternatives.
- Tone constraints.

If proof is missing, write lower-claim copy and mark where proof is needed.

## Build The Argument

Create the page argument before writing polished prose:

- Reader arrives with this belief.
- The page must change that belief to this.
- The strongest promise is this.
- The proof is this.
- The next action is this.

Every section should move the reader closer to the action. Remove claims that do not support the argument.

## Match Claims To Proof

For every meaningful claim, define:

- Claim.
- Proof type: customer quote, metric, demo, screenshot, credential, example, guarantee, comparison.
- Proof strength: weak, usable, strong, publishable.
- Allowed wording ceiling: what can be said honestly without overstating.
- Placement: hero, proof block, FAQ, CTA area, pricing, section body.
- Gap: what proof is missing.

## Write The Copy

Use:

- Specific outcomes over soft adjectives.
- Concrete nouns and verbs.
- Customer language where available.
- Short sections with one job each.
- CTAs that state what happens next.
- Objection handling near the point of hesitation.

Avoid:

- Empty superiority claims.
- Unsupported numbers.
- Clever lines that hide the offer.
- Exclamation marks as intensity.
- Feature dumps without user consequence.

## Page Notes

- **Homepage:** explain the product and create clear paths for different visitors.
- **Landing page:** one audience, one promise, one primary CTA.
- **Pricing page:** help the buyer choose, reduce risk, and explain plan differences.
- **Feature/product page:** connect feature, use case, proof, and outcome.
- **About page:** tell the story only where it strengthens trust or conversion.
